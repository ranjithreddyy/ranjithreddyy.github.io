What AI Changed · #1 · Posted Nov 8, 2026

An amateur found a possible planet in NASA data. With AI agents.

No telescope. No astronomy degree. Pavel Rabtsevich, a 28-year-old product manager in Spain, pointed AI coding agents at free TESS light curves for 126,246 stars — and one star, 116 light-years away, dims 0.05% for ~2 hours every 3.18 days, right on schedule across 2018, 2020 and 2025 data.

He then ran 74 analyses designed to prove himself wrong — and published his predicted dip times before MIT's follow-up observations. If it's real: a hot super-Earth, ~1.4× Earth's size, found from a laptop.

Original post on LinkedIn ↗
  1. The setup — NASA's TESS telescope publishes star data for free. TESS finds planets by watching for stars that dim on a schedule.
  2. The method — AI agents wrote the search code, fitted transits and reran what failed. Claude Code did much of the work; other agents cross-checked it.
  3. The signal — TIC 4206066: 23 transits, ~0.05% dips, every 3.18 days, across three observation years.
  4. The tests — Strongest check: hide one year of data and predict its dips from the other two. The prediction landed 3 out of 3.
  5. What could still be wrong — False-positive probability ~3–4%. It is a candidate, not a confirmed planet — and he says so himself.
  6. The approval — MIT granted Director's Discretionary Target time, with him as principal investigator. The search got cheaper. The standard of proof did not change.
